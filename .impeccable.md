# Design Context

## Users
Families planning the next few days of home cooking: breakfast, lunch, snack and dinner. Often a parent on a phone in the kitchen or on the sofa, sometimes at a laptop on a Sunday filling the week. They want to answer "what are we eating?" quickly and keep an eye on calories and macros without it feeling like a fitness tracker.

## Use cases
- Fill meal slots for the visible days from the shared recipe library
- Move meals around (drag and drop) as plans change
- Check whether each day is on track against daily calorie/macro targets
- Maintain the recipe library (add, edit, import/export)
- Ask Claude to suggest meals for a week and pick the ones to keep

## Brand personality
Warm kitchen-table. Feels like a family's fridge planner written on good paper, not an admin dashboard.
- Cream/paper neutrals tinted warm, ink-brown text, terracotta as the single strong accent
- A characterful serif for display (Young Serif), a friendly humanist sans for UI (Figtree)
- Food-led meal colours: breakfast = yolk, lunch = sage, snack = plum, dinner = dusk
- Copy in a plain family voice ("Suggest meals", "Sign out", "Nothing planned"), no tech jargon
- Calm feedback: changes show in place; toasts only for undo or failures
